The Driving Point of Oscillation and the Instantaneous Center of Rotation in a Uniform Wooden Rod
Jungwoo Lee
IB Extended Essay, Physics HL (Rigid Body Dynamics), 2026

Inspired by the "sweet spot" of a baseball bat, this essay asks how the position of the driving point of oscillation (xosc) determines the position of the instantaneous center of rotation (xICR) in a 100 cm uniform pine rod. Treating small-amplitude oscillation as a rapid succession of impacts, I derived xICR = −(L2/12)·xosc−1 from the superposition of translational and rotational motion. I built a wooden support frame and an intermediate linkage driven by a linear actuator (2.6–3.4 Hz), filmed nine driving points at 60 fps, and tracked 19 holes along the rod in Tracker. Fourier spectra isolated the driving frequency, sinusoidal regression gave each hole's amplitude, and the zero-amplitude intercept located the ICR. The data follow xICR = (−924 ± 198)·xosc−1.08 ± 0.09 (R2 ≈ 0.998), consistent with the predicted inverse law (exponent −1, constant −833), with the pivot always on the opposite side of the center of mass. The same principle lets a robotic arm strike or capture objects at its center of percussion to minimize shock transmitted to its joints and base.

Phase Portraits of a One-Degree-of-Freedom Quarter-Car Suspension
Jungwoo Lee
IB Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation HL Internal Assessment, 2026

Motivated by how differently a car and a bus ride over the same speed bump, this exploration models one wheel of a car as a 1-DOF quarter-car (m = 400 kg, k = 10 000 N m−1) released 0.1 m above equilibrium. I rewrote mẍ + cẋ + kx = 0 as a coupled first-order system X′ = MX and used its characteristic equation λ2 + (c/400)λ + 25 = 0 to show that the suspension is stable for every c > 0 and is critically damped at c = 4000 N s m−1. Solving exactly with eigenvalues and eigenvectors for critically damped (λ = −5, repeated), over-damped (λ = −2.5, −10) and under-damped (ζ = 0.3, λ ≈ −1.5 ± 4.77i) suspensions, I drew their phase portraits in Python. They show a degenerate node, a node whose final approach follows the slow eigenvector v = −2.5x, and a clockwise spiral that keeps only about 14% of its amplitude after each oscillation, which is the repeated bouncing I felt on the bus. Which damping is "best" depends on whether settling time, overshoot or comfort is being optimized.

Volumetric Flow Rate of Water and Pipe Length and Radius under Constant Hydrostatic Pressure
Jungwoo Lee
IB Physics HL Internal Assessment, 2026

Noticing that different café straws deliver very different amounts of drink, I measured how the volumetric flow rate Q of water depends on the radius (1–3 mm) and length (10–50 cm) of acrylic pipes. A custom-built, continuously overflowing acrylic tank held the water head, and therefore the driving pressure, constant, and Q was found by weighing the water discharged in 30 s. Combining the two power laws gave Q = 10.71·r2.3 ± 0.8/L0.38 ± 0.05 (R2 = 0.997 for the combined fit). Compared with a turbulent model derived from the Darcy–Weisbach equation and the Blasius correlation (Q ∝ r2.71L−0.571), the radius exponent agreed within uncertainty but the length exponent differed significantly. Calculating the Reynolds numbers (about 1200–6500) explained why: the flows spanned laminar, transitional and turbulent regimes, so no single-regime model applies, and I proposed how to redesign the apparatus to keep every pipe in one regime.

The Effect of Temperature on the Equilibrium Constant of Iron(III) Thiocyanate
Jungwoo Lee
IB Chemistry HL Internal Assessment, 2026

This investigation measures how temperature (10–50 °C) affects the equilibrium constant of Fe3+(aq) + SCN−(aq) ⇌ [Fe(SCN)]2+(aq). I built a Beer–Lambert calibration curve at 458 nm with a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (ε = 4790 ± 270 dm3 mol−1 cm−1), added nitric acid to suppress Fe3+ hydrolysis, and measured equilibrium mixtures as they cooled or warmed through each target temperature. Kc fell from 310 ± 50 at 11.2 °C to 200 ± 30 at 49.8 °C, as Le Chatelier's principle predicts for an exothermic forward reaction, and a van 't Hoff plot of ln Kc against 1/T was linear (R2 = 0.97). The absolute values were about twice the literature value at 20 °C; I traced this systematic error mainly to moisture absorbed by aged reagents and to gas bubbles released on heating, and proposed specific improvements.

China's Anti-Dumping Tariffs on EU Pork and Cross-Sector Interdependence
Jungwoo Lee
IB Economics SL Internal Assessment, Commentary 3 (Global Economics), 2026
source article (Reuters)

Using tariff diagrams, this commentary compares China's preliminary (up to 62.4%) and final (mostly 9.8%) anti-dumping duties on EU pork, showing how the smaller tariff shrinks every welfare effect. I argue that the tariff moved more with the separate EU–China dispute over electric-vehicle tariffs than with the pork market itself: interdependence can de-escalate two conflicts at once, but it shifts risk onto exporters.
